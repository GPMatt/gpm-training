Orientation

What the system does, in one pass

Employees buy things on the company credit card and submit a photo of each receipt through a Google Form. Once a month the bank sends one statement PDF listing every charge on that card. This system's whole job is to pair each submitted receipt with the real charge that matches it, then bill the property owner for it in QuickBooks: one invoice per owner, with the receipt images attached.

Who does what

  • Laura runs the full cycle, Invoice & Send included.
  • Matt backs her up, and gets every operational alert.
  • Alaina gets the per-owner summary and the receipt packet after each cycle.

The three tabs you actually work in

Submissions is the permanent ledger: one row per receipt, forever. Every row carries a Status in col O, and that status decides whether it bills. This is where fixes happen.

Reconciliation is a scratch pad, rebuilt from zero every time you parse a statement or re-run matching. One row per charge on the card, each with one of four statuses.

Needs a Look pkg 4 is your to-do list for the cycle. It's rebuilt on every Re-run and lists only open items, each with the reason and the fix. When the only lines left on it are Missing receipts, you can bill.

Non-negotiable

The five standing rules

Everything below follows from these. If something you're about to do would break one, stop and ask Matt instead.

1 · Fix the detail, never approve pkg 1

There is no Approve tick on the purchases tab any more. When a charge is flagged, something on the receipt row is wrong: the amount, the date, the store, or it's a duplicate. Correct that detail, then Re-run Matching. A clean re-run is what clears the flag. You never wave a flag through.

2 · Nothing bills without a matching statement charge

A receipt only becomes billable once the system has found the actual bank charge behind it. This is what stops GPM invoicing an owner for a purchase the card was never charged for. There is no hand-typed exception any more: a Ready row that no matched charge points at is set back to Pending on the next Re-run. pkg 4 See no charge at all →

3 · The billed amount is always Submissions col K

Amount (Parsed) is a human-entered number. No automatic process ever calculates it, adjusts it, or copies the bank's figure into it. The one tool that changes it from the Reconciliation tab is Override Amount →, and it can only correct a receipt to exactly the card charge, and only on that cardholder's own Pending receipt. pkg 1

4 · "Col K" means two different things. Always say which tab

Naming hazard On Submissions, col K is the dollar amount that gets billed. On Reconciliation, col K is the link that opens the receipt photo. They are unrelated. Never say "col K" without naming the tab.

5 · There are two Lauras

Laura (president) runs the cycle and is on the summary emails and the CC Laura checkbox. Laura C is a maintenance tech and an ordinary cardholder like anyone else. On the statement she prints differently. Don't merge them on the Cardholders tab.

Known issue Drive's "CC Receipts — By Cardholder" folder has two folders for the tech: Laura-C and Laura C. Billing isn't affected (attachments are fetched by file, not by folder). Being looked into. Don't merge or rename them by hand.

The monthly cycle · Steps 1–8

Start to finish

Do these in order. Steps 4 and 5 loop (fix, re-run, fix again) and that loop is free. Everything before Step 7 can be undone or repeated. Step 7 cannot.

STEP 1

Run Health Check

GPM Billing → Health Check · free · read-only

Safe to run any time, as often as you like. It writes nothing. Read the whole report before you go further. Several problems only ever show up here.

The last line tells you where you stand:

  • === INTAKE READY ===: the blocking checks passed. Warnings above may still deserve attention.
  • === ACTION REQUIRED ===: something blocking is wrong. Do not proceed.
The single most dangerous setting: TEST_EMAIL_OVERRIDE

TEST_EMAIL_OVERRIDE on the Config tab reroutes every email (owner invoices, chase emails, the receipt packet) to one address. If it is set while TEST_MODE is FALSE, a real cycle sends every owner's invoice to that one inbox and reports complete success. Nothing errors. You find out when an owner asks where their bill is.

For a real run: TEST_MODE = FALSE, TEST_EMAIL_OVERRIDE and TEST_ROWS blank. Health Check refuses to pass while that combination is wrong, and the Invoice & Send popup shows TEST mode if it's on. Believe both.

STEP 3

Part 1 — Parse Statement

GPM Billing → Part 1 — Parse Statement · free on the normal path

Two parsers: one free, one failsafe

Parse Statement tries a deterministic parser first: a Cloud Run service that reads the PDF's text columns with fixed rules. No AI, no cost, well under a second. It then checks its own answer against the statement's printed numbers: the purchases total, the credits total, and a subtotal for every individual card. That's about 28 checks on a typical statement, and all of them must pass before its answer is trusted.

Claude only runs as the failsafe: when the service is unreachable or one of those checks fails (almost always because the bank changed the statement's layout). That's the one case that costs an API call and takes a minute or two. Either way, the report and everything after it are identical. (The menu label still says "1 API call". On the normal path it's free.)

Then a report appears. Read it. Don't just click OK.

In the reportWhat you're checking
Parser The first line. PARSED BY: the deterministic parser (Cloud Run). No model was used. is the normal case. *** PARSED BY: CLAUDE (FALLBACK) *** means the service couldn't vouch for itself this month. The reason is printed right there, and an error report is filed automatically.
Charge total check DELTA: $0.00 ✓ exact means every charge was read and they sum to the printed purchases total. Anything else and the parse already refused itself. See here →
Cardholders found Mostly a worry on a Claude-fallback parse. The cardholder is a heading, not a column, so it has to be carried across page breaks. A mistake doesn't error. It puts charges on the wrong person, and Step 6 then emails them about it. You want ✓ every statement name matches a cardholder. On the deterministic path this is already one of its self-checks.
First 10 charges Spot-check these against the statement, line by line. Dates, amounts, vendor text.
Credits / refunds Refunds are read separately from purchases. If the count looks wrong, or the self-consistency warning appears, don't keep the parse.

Click it once per statement. To re-match after fixing things, that's Step 5, which never re-reads the PDF.

The OK / Cancel decision

OK keeps the parse and immediately runs the first match. The Statements row stays, the Reconciliation and Needs a Look tabs fill in, and the PDF moves to Processed.

Cancel undoes all of it: the Statements row is deleted, Reconciliation is cleared, and the PDF goes back where you found it. On the normal path a re-parse costs nothing. On a Claude-fallback parse the API call is spent either way.

Why the dialog can feel slow to appear Everything is written to the sheet before you're asked, on purpose: the script has a hard six-minute limit, and a dialog waiting on a person burns that clock.

STEP 5

Part 1 — Re-run Matching

GPM Billing → Part 1 — Re-run Matching (free) · free · repeat freely

Click this after every round of fixes. It costs nothing, calls nothing outside the sheet, and you can run it twenty times in an afternoon. It reads the charges already on the Reconciliation tab. It does not re-read the PDF.

What one run does, in order:

  1. Applies any Override Amount you typed, writing it into Submissions col K first, so the corrected figure matches on this same run.
  2. Re-matches everything from scratch. The closest date wins across all of a person's charges, so the order of rows on the tab never changes the result. pkg 3
  3. Promotes every Matched receipt from Pending to Ready.
  4. Sets any orphan Ready row back to Pending and emails Matt the list. pkg 4
  5. Rewrites the Reconciliation and Needs a Look tabs and shows you a report.

Read the report's top section first

Under --- Your decisions --- the report lists every Override Amount as APPLIED or REJECTED, with the reason. A rejected override is one you think you already made, so it's listed first. The reason is also written on the row. confirm report wording after pkg 1

An override is used up whether or not it worked Once a run reads Override Amount, the cell is cleared, accepted or rejected. It can never be applied twice by accident. If yours was rejected, read why, fix the cause, and type it again.
You're ready for Step 6 when The Needs a Look tab lists nothing but Missing receipts.
STEP 6

Send Chase Emails

GPM Billing → Send Chase Emails · emails real people · no undo

Highest blast radius in the project One click emails every cardholder who has a charge with no receipt. It relies on the cardholder headings being read correctly in Step 3. If one was carried wrong, this emails Riley about John's charges, to their real inbox, with no undo. Finish Steps 4 and 5 before you touch this.

It's a separate button on purpose. You'll re-run matching several times in one sitting, and bundling emails into that would nag everyone three times an afternoon.

A confirmation box appears first

  • Every recipient name and the exact address it's going to
  • How many charges and what dollar total each person is being chased for
  • Anyone marked (NO EMAIL — will be skipped)
  • Whether TEST_EMAIL_OVERRIDE is diverting everything

Read the names against the statement before clicking OK. That's the whole point of the box. Cancel sends nothing.

What actually goes out Each cardholder gets their own email, Receipt needed — <period>, listing only their own charges. Laura and Jason get one combined digest, Missing receipts — <period>, grouped by person. A cardholder with no email on the Cardholders tab is skipped and appears only in that digest.
Missing receipts never block billing You don't wait for replies. Nothing bills off a Missing receipt, so it never stops Invoice & Send. Receipts that come in later ride the normal flow: if they're inside this statement's window they match on the next Re-run; if the statement has closed by then they land as Prior Period.
STEP 7

Part 2 — Invoice & Send

GPM Billing → Part 2 — Invoice & Send · creates real invoices · no undo

This is the irreversible one It creates real QuickBooks invoices and emails them to real property owners. There is no undo and no draft state. Everything before this point was reversible; nothing after it is.

The pre-flight popup pkg 4

Clicking the menu item opens a popup first. Nothing is sent until you press its button.

  • "Will invoice N owners, $X total", then one line per owner with their total.
  • TEST mode, shown plainly if it's on.
  • Blockers, in plain words. While anything on Needs a Look is still open (other than Missing receipts), the Send these invoices button isn't there. The list of blockers is shown in its place. Close the popup, fix them, Re-run Matching, and try again.
  • With nothing blocking, check the owners and totals look right, then click Send these invoices.

What it will bill

Every Submissions row that is Ready at the moment you click Send, nothing else. Not Pending, not Error. That set is frozen when the cycle starts, so a receipt submitted while it's running waits for next month.

Other things that refuse before anything happens

  1. QuickBooks isn't connected: a missing credential. Matt's job.
  2. The test-mode guard: see the red box in Step 1.
  3. No reconciled statement: the Reconciliation tab must hold charges from the newest Statements row. This stops this month's receipts being billed against last month's numbers.
  4. Nothing is Ready: go back to Steps 4 and 5.

Then it runs for 20–30 minutes, mostly without you

Google caps any single script run at six minutes. So it bills one owner per run and schedules itself again about a minute later, until it's done. Those follow-on runs skip the popup (there's no screen for them).

Do not click it again while it's running

You'll see a toast reading "Billing started — N owners…", then progress toasts. You can close the sheet. It keeps running on Google's servers and emails you when it finishes. A second click mid-cycle is caught by a lock, but don't rely on it.

What each owner gets

  • One invoice per owner, not per property. Each property is its own group of lines on that invoice.
  • Each line shows the property address (and unit), then the itemized description.
  • Every receipt photo is attached, all pages.
  • It goes to the owner's AppFolio email from PropertyMap col D, copying Alaina, and copying Laura when CC Laura is checked on any of their properties.

A refund row (negative amount) becomes a Credit Memo rather than an invoice line. Detail →

As each row finishes

It turns Sent with the invoice number in col P, or Error with the reason in col R. An Error row here is a dollar that's stuck, not a dollar that's lost. Recovering it →

Situations & exceptions

When it isn't the ordinary case

Each of these is linked from the step where you'd hit it, and reads on its own.

Needs a look: reasons and fixes pkg 1 · 3

A charge reads "Needs a look" and is listed on the Needs a Look tab

These used to be six separate statuses (Near miss, Over charge, Double Matched, Merchant Mismatch, Date Mismatch, Possible duplicate) and most had an Approve tick. Now they're one status, and every one clears the same way: find the wrong detail, correct it, Re-run Matching. Amounts never legitimately differ. A gap means a typo or a split.

Receipt a few cents off, or bigger than the charge

Usually a typo when the amount was entered ($95.36 for $95.37, or $910.50 for $91.05). Open the photo. Then type the exact charge into Override Amount →. An owner is never billed more than the card was charged.

If the charge is more than the receipt (often an unprinted tip), the fix is the same: Override Amount to the exact charge, so the owner is billed what the card paid.

Same amount, date outside the window

The amount and person agree to the cent, but the receipt is dated more than 7 days before or 4 days after the charge. Nothing is paired until it's fixed. Check the date on the photo and correct it on Submissions. If the photo really is from a different day, it's a different purchase: leave it and treat the charge as Missing receipt.

A split: one purchase, several properties

One card charge, several receipt rows (one form per property) that add up to it. It matches automatically only when all of these hold: same person, the rows add up to the cent, every date in the window, same store, and no other combination of that person's receipts also adds up. The same rule covers one Amazon order charged in pieces.

If it didn't match, the note names the rows that nearly add up. Fix the detail that's off, most often a blank store in col T or one row's amount or date.

Look-alikes (two receipts, one charge)

Same person, same amount, same date, same store, and both rows are shown. The system won't guess which is real. Open both photos:

  • The same receipt submitted twice: set the re-submission to Non-Billable with Set Status for Row(s)….
  • Two genuinely identical purchases (memberships often bill several identical amounts on one day): the second charge hasn't shown up. Chase it, or wait for next month's statement.

Store names disagree

Amount, person and date line up, but the vendor names don't. Store numbers, city suffixes, LLC/INC and prefixes like SQ * are already ignored, so a disagreement that survives is worth a look. If the store on Submissions col T is wrong, correct it. If both are right and it's simply an alias (RYLEE'S ACE HARDWARE ↔ Great Lakes Ace Hardware), add the pairing to the Merchant Map. DOLLAR-GENERAL and Family Dollar are different stores and should stay flagged. confirm with pkg 3 build

Several suggestions, different amounts

When nothing matches exactly, the note lists up to three ranked suggestions. A suggestion never uses a receipt up, so the same receipt can still match another charge. Pick the right one from the photos and fix its amount with Override Amount.

How Override Amount works pkg 1

Reconciliation col N

Type a figure into col N on the charge's row and Re-run Matching. It writes that figure into Submissions col K before matching, so the corrected receipt matches on the same run. It's accepted only if all of these hold, and refused with the reason written on the row otherwise:

  1. It equals exactly the charge amount on that row.
  2. col H names exactly one Submissions row.
  3. That row was submitted by this charge's cardholder. (A refusal names both people, for example "row 162 is Jody's receipt, this is Blake's card".)
  4. That row is Pending.
  5. That row is dated on or before this statement's last transaction. confirm still enforced

Accepted or refused, the cell is then cleared. If yours was refused, fix the cause and type it again.

Parse Statement refused

"Parse Statement REFUSED — …"

Five refusals, all checked before any money is spent:

  • More than one PDF in the folder: move all but the current statement out.
  • This exact PDF is already recorded: you've already parsed it. Use Re-run Matching instead.
  • The newest Statements row already covers this period: a re-download of the same statement is a different Drive file, so this catches what the check above can't. If it genuinely is a new statement, correct or delete the offending Statements row.
  • Open/close dates didn't read as a valid period: nothing was written, the PDF stayed put. Re-run; if it repeats, tell Matt.
  • Charges don't add up to the printed total: see below.

The delta isn't $0.00

"✗ HARD FAIL — charges were dropped or misread"

The parsed charges don't sum to the purchases total printed on the statement. This is a hard refusal: lines were dropped or misread, and everything downstream depends on that list being complete.

Nothing was written and the PDF was left where it is. Run Parse Statement again. If it fails twice, send Matt the report text. Don't try to correct the tab by hand.

Missing receipt, and suppressing the ones that don't need chasing

Dozens of charges reading "Missing receipt"

Most of what's on the card isn't billable to any owner: fuel, car washes, Comcast, Zoom, Intuit, subscriptions, restaurants, parking. Left alone, every one reads Missing receipt and Step 6 chases someone for it. They never block Invoice & Send.

The fix: a noReceipt rule

Add a row to the noReceipt tab:

col A Cardholder: the employee name or the printed statement name. Both work.
col B Vendor: matched by containment against the statement's vendor text.
col C Amount, optional. Blank covers any amount; a figure covers only that exact one.

A charge a rule covers reads No bill and is never chased.

Keep vendor strings specific Because it matches by containment, a vague string covers more than you intend. Prefer MISTER CAR WASH over WASH. Anything under three characters is rejected. A short but specific name like VIOC is fine.

These rules are permanent. There's no monthly cleanup

ADD When a new recurring vendor shows up as Missing receipt noise that nobody should ever be chased for. A handful of rows a year, at most.

Rows worth an occasional second look are stores that sell both groceries and property supplies: MEIJER, AMAZON, AUTOZONE, DOLLAR-GENERAL. Vehicle-service rows (VIOC, MUFFLER MAN, KOOL CHEVROLET) are safely permanent.

How the report tells you a rule got too broad A noReceipt warning appears when one rule covered two or more different merchants on one statement, and names them so you can check none should have been chased.
The one narrow risk of an exemption Exemptions only apply to charges nothing matched, so a rule can't hide a matched receipt. But if a receipt exists and fails to match (wrong amount, date outside the window), a rule can make its charge read No bill instead of Missing receipt. The receipt isn't lost: it's still in the unmatched block below the charges. Scan that block every cycle.

Refunds and credits

The Reconciliation Credits tab fills in

A refund is submitted through the same form with a negative amount. Statement credits are read separately from purchases and matched on their own tab.

This tab keeps its Approve tick, and every credit needs one A purchase checks itself: the itemized receipt has to add up to the billed amount. A credit has no such check, so your approval is the safety rail. Nothing on this tab is processed automatically, however good the match.

The two proposals

  • "Refund — would net, no bill": exactly one unbilled purchase for the same owner matches the credit to the cent. Approving marks both rows Netted — No Bill. Neither bills; no QuickBooks document is created.
  • "Refund — would credit": no single clean offset. Approving promotes the row to Ready, and Step 7 creates a Credit Memo in QuickBooks.
Netted purchases stay put now pkg 3 A purchase marked Netted — No Bill now reads No bill on the purchases tab and only matches its own charge exactly. Before, it could grab unrelated receipts and shuffle them between runs (Blake's $157.94 Amazon refund, August).
Credit Memos show as "unsent" in QuickBooks. That's correct A Credit Memo is never emailed to the owner. It sits open and comes off that owner's next invoice automatically, as long as Automatically apply credits is on in QuickBooks Advanced settings.

Credits have no earliest-date limit, on purpose: a refund often posts a statement or two after the purchase it reverses.

The matching rules, in order

  1. Who's in the pool. Every Pending receipt can match. Receipts that are already billed (Sent), netted, GPM-paid, Prior Period or in Error can match too, but only exactly: same person, same cents, date in the window. That keeps their charges from reading Missing receipt every month, without letting them grab someone else's charge.
  2. The window. A receipt can be dated up to 7 days before the charge's transaction date, or up to 4 days after.
  3. Exact matches, closest date wins. Same cardholder, same cents, inside the window. When one receipt could fit more than one of that person's charges, the closest date wins across all of them, so the order of rows never changes the answer. When an unbilled and an already-billed receipt tie, the unbilled one wins.
  4. Look-alikes are held. Two receipts with the same person, amount, date and store → Needs a look, both rows shown, until one is fixed.
  5. Splits, exact only. 2–4 of one person's receipts that add up to the cent, all dated in the window, same store, and no other combination also adds up → Matched. Anything less → Needs a look with the rows named. Same for one order charged in pieces.
  6. Same cents, outside the window → Needs a look with "fix the date". Nothing is paired.
  7. Different amounts → Needs a look with up to 3 ranked suggestions. A suggestion uses nothing up.
  8. Nothing at all. Only now is a noReceipt exemption checked, so a rule can never hide a real receipt. A hit → No bill. No hit → Missing receipt.
